Skip it, for most people. *Process Over Magic* is a short, sane argument for owning your code and not outsourcing your thinking to an AI. Barbini, a longtime TDD advocate, wants you to treat AI assistants as tools you stay in control of: plan, take small steps, review the output, own the result. It's all reasonable. It's also nothing an experienced developer doesn't already know.

The problem is the examples. The book teaches through worked sessions in Windsurf, an editor I don't know a single person still using, and it keeps noting that the assistant isn't good enough to do this or that yet. Then Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2, and Gemini 3 all landed within weeks of each other in late 2025, and most of those *yet*s became routine. He was describing a ceiling that is now a floor.

There's an irony there: a book called *Process Over Magic* is weakest on the magic. The process advice survives; the predictions about the magic aged badly. Which leaves it for almost nobody. The experienced won't learn anything, and the beginners who might can't tell which parts have rotted.

It isn't really the author's fault. The space moves too fast for print, and by the time a book ships the ground has shifted under it. The fast channels are where you keep up now. The one thing worth keeping is also the one that doesn't expire: own your code, and don't outsource the thinking. You may not read every line an assistant writes anymore, but you should always know what's in the code you ship.
